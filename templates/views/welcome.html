The project

What this project does

This simulator models warehouse operations so you can configure a scenario, run it, inspect the results, and compare decisions with reproducible data. The goal is to make warehouse performance easier to explore, explain, and improve in an educational and research setting.

The beginning

Inspiration

I first encountered warehouse operations and layout in early 2025 while learning about plant layout and material handling. Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) immediately caught my attention. Around the same time, control theory introduced me to Brian Douglas from MATLAB and his work on swarm robotics: the idea that simple rules and hardware can produce powerful collective behavior.

I originally imagined a research proposal around that idea, but a physical implementation was far beyond my budget as a student. I began learning simulation instead, moving from a spring-mass model in C and raylib, to heat transfer in Python, and then to a bottled-water production model. Each project taught me something, even when the data or the question was not quite ready.

This warehouse project brought those threads together. My focus is less on AMR physics or decentralised control for now, and more on how different parameters affect warehouse performance and how to optimise it. Swarm behaviour, user-designed layouts, and deeper decision support are directions I hope to add in the future.
